Equilibrium and non-equilibrium analysis
----------------------------------------

The examples provided in this section demonstrate the functionality of MatCalc for thermodynamic equilibrium analysis. The first examples demonstrate basic features and give instructions on how to perform typical calculations. These are the generation of stable and metastable phase fraction diagrams, as well as chemical driving forces. The later examples show how to use this module of MatCalc to retrieve estimates of how the microstructure of a material could look like and which precipitates one can expect to see in experiments. This type of analysis is often applied for temperature cycles with low to moderate temperature gradients.

* **E1** -  [Stable and meta-stable phase diagram of Fe-C-Mn](/examples/equilib/e1 "examples/equilib/e1") - Evaluate ortho-equilibrium and para-equilibrium phase fraction and phase diagrams.
* **E2** -  [Stable and meta-stable phases in Al-Cu](/examples/equilib/e2 "examples/equilib/e2") - Plot phase diagram for stable Theta-phase as well as metastable GP-zone, Theta“ and Theta' phase precipitation.
* **E3** -  [Analysis of the miscibility gap in Fe-C-N-Nb-V](/examples/equilib/e3 "examples/equilib/e3") - Create and manipulate composition sets to analyze complex miscibility gaps.
* **E6** -  [Composition-dependent driving forces for carbide precipitation in Fe-C-Cr](/examples/equilib/e6 "examples/equilib/e6") - Evaluate the driving force for precipitation as a function of the precipitate composition.
* **E10** -  ['Most-likely' nucleus composition for bcc-Cu precipitation in bcc-Fe](/examples/equilib/e10 "examples/equilib/e10") - Determine the critical nucleus composition for minimum nucleation barrier from thermodynamic quantities.
* **E20** -  [Thermodynamic equilibrium analysis for continuous casting simulation of micro-alloyed Fe-Al-C-N-Nb-Ti steel](/examples/equilib/e20 "examples/equilib/e20") - This analysis is a recommended step before carrying out precipitation kinetics simulations in inhomogeneous, segregated primary solidification microstructure.
